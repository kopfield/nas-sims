Understanding Wave Properties

Amplitude (A): The maximum displacement or distance moved by a point on a vibrating body or wave measured from its equilibrium position. It corresponds to the "height" of the wave.

Wavelength (λ): The spatial period of the periodic wave – the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is the distance between consecutive corresponding points of the same phase, such as two adjacent crests or troughs.

Frequency (f): The number of occurrences of a repeating event per unit of time. For waves, it is the number of crests (or any other point of the wave) that pass a point per unit of time. It is measured in Hertz (Hz).

Wave Speed (v): The speed at which a wave propagates. It is related to frequency and wavelength by the fundamental wave equation: v = f × λ.
